model, not adviceverify with a CPArelocation modeledhigh-earner tax Simplifications: depreciation = (basis − land) ÷ 27.5 (capped at the building value); high-earner rental losses are suspended and released against the sale gain; §121 excludes appreciation if sold within your window, while depreciation is always recaptured (unrecaptured §1250) at up to 25% federal, plus NIIT and state. Commission/closing/transfer/concessions reduce the taxable gain; repairs/staging reduce cash but not the gain. Mortgage interest is deductible and amortized; principal builds equity. The 3.8% NIIT, when enabled, is applied to the capital gain, the recapture, AND any positive net rental profit (it's net investment income for high earners). The "ordinary rate" is your FEDERAL marginal rate; the state field covers the gain and recapture. One gap: state income tax on positive annual rental profit isn't separately broken out — immaterial while the rental runs at a tax loss (the usual early-year case), but nudge the ordinary rate up a few points if you want to capture it in profitable years. Confirm specifics with a tax professional.

Relocation benefits: each path picks its own package because they are asymmetric. A full-service package's home-selling coverage (commission/closing/transfer/etc.) only has value if you actually sell, so on the sell side it is added back as a credit against those exact costs — the costs still show as negatives, the credit nets them toward zero. The MEA is netted at your ordinary rate when marked taxable. A lump sum is cash either way: it boosts sale proceeds on the sell side or is invested on the rent side. The model treats full-service reimbursements as roughly tax-neutral via the employer gross-up; in reality gross-up is imperfect, so treat the sell-side benefit as a near-upper-bound. Clawback: set your expected months at the new employer; if that falls inside the package's window (24 months full-service, 12 months lump sum), the unserved fraction of the benefit is prorated and subtracted from that path, since you would owe it back on an early exit. The clawback keys off job tenure, not how long you hold the house.
